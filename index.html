We are looking at how basic research is funded. Who performs the research is different: academic institutions are the largest performers of federally funded basic research.

The important missing piece is consumption and impact. It is very hard to track who uses federally funded basic research and to measure its impact.

The government basic research portfolio looks a lot like the industry portfolio.

The overlap coefficient in 2023 was 70%, meaning 70 cents out of every dollar of funding are spent on a similar portfolio.

Life sciences is the largest single field for each.

The federal government puts more emphasis on the physical sciences and the environment.

The private sector puts more emphasis on engineering and computer sciences.

The FY2026 President’s Budget Request proposed deep cuts to federal basic research.

Congress restored the cuts and added a small amount, barely beating inflation: the 4% nominal increase is 1.8% in real terms.

The FY2027 President’s Budget Request, released April 3, 2026, proposed deep to moderate cuts to the topline discretionary budgets of NSF, EPA, NASA, Agriculture, HHS, Commerce, and Energy, but did not provide specific details on R&D. As of September 2026, the government is operating under a continuing resolution with no FY2027 appropriations bills enacted.

It remains to be seen how Congress will carry forward the legacy of federal support for basic research.
